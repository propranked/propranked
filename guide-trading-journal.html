Trading Journal: Your Secret Weapon for Success

Every professional trader keeps a trading journal. It's not optional—it's mandatory if you want to improve and pass prop firm evaluations. Your journal is where you analyze your trading, identify patterns, and prevent repeating mistakes.

Why Traders Keep Journals

Without a journal, you're flying blind. You might think you're doing great, but in reality, you're probably repeating the same mistakes. A journal helps you:

  • Track your win rate and profitability
  • Identify your best trading times
  • Find patterns in your losing trades
  • Monitor your emotional control
  • Measure improvement over time

What to Track in Your Journal

Basic Trade Information:
• Date and time of entry
• Market (ES, NQ, EURUSD, etc.)
• Entry price and stop loss
• Exit price and profit/loss
• Position size
• Duration (how long you held the trade)

Setup Information:
• Why you entered (support, breakout, pattern, etc.)
• Setup type (scalp, swing, breakout, reversal)
• Risk-reward ratio
• Market context (trending, choppy, etc.)

Emotional/Behavioral Data:
• Your confidence level (1-10)
• If you followed your plan
• Any emotional triggers
• Lessons learned

Sample Journal Entry

Date: March 25, 2026 | 9:35 AM EST

Market: ES (E-mini S&P 500)

Entry: 5,195 | Stop: 5,190 (5 point stop)

Exit: 5,200 | P/L: +$250 (2 contracts)

Duration: 8 minutes

Setup: Breakout above resistance + volume confirmation

Risk-Reward: 1:2

Confidence: 8/10 - Good setup, solid risk management

Notes: Followed plan perfectly. Great execution. This is the type of trade to replicate.

How to Analyze Your Journal

Every week, review your trades and look for patterns:

  • Win Rate: What percentage of your trades are profitable?
  • Best Time to Trade: When are you most profitable?
  • Losing Trade Patterns: What do your losses have in common?
  • Setup Profitability: Which setups work best for you?
  • Emotional Patterns: When do you make impulsive trades?

Digital vs Paper Journaling

Digital (Spreadsheet): Easier to search and analyze. Good for finding patterns. Most professional traders use spreadsheets (Excel, Google Sheets).

Paper: Forces deeper reflection. Slower, but you remember trades better. Good supplement to digital journaling.

Recommendation: Use a spreadsheet for systematic tracking, plus a paper journal for reflections and lessons learned.

Journal Template

Create a simple Excel/Google Sheets with these columns:

  • Date
  • Time
  • Market
  • Entry Price
  • Stop Loss
  • Exit Price
  • Profit/Loss
  • Setup Type
  • Risk-Reward
  • Win (Y/N)
  • Notes

The 21-Trade Rule

Don't evaluate your trading based on a single trade or even a day. Look at your last 21 trades. If your win rate is above 50% with positive profit, you're trading profitably. Most professional traders aim for 50-60% win rate with good risk-reward ratios.

Bottom Line

Your trading journal is your roadmap to improvement. Most traders who fail to improve skip journaling. The ones who get better are the ones who review every trade. If you want to pass prop firm evaluations and become a consistently profitable trader, keep a journal religiously.

Make it non-negotiable: Every trade gets journaled. Every week gets reviewed. This is how professionals separate themselves from amateurs.
