Trading Journal: Your Secret Weapon

Track every trade, identify your patterns, and accelerate your improvement as a prop trader.

The single most common trait among consistently profitable traders is that they keep a trading journal. Not occasionally — every single day, every single trade. Here's why it matters and how to do it right.

Why a Trading Journal is Non-Negotiable

Without a journal, you're flying blind. You might feel like you're improving, but feelings aren't data. A journal gives you objective feedback on what's actually working and what's costing you money.

Traders who journal consistently report identifying losing patterns within 30 days that they had been repeating for months. The journal doesn't change your behavior — it makes the truth undeniable.

What to Record in Every Entry

FieldWhat to Record
Date & TimeWhen the trade was entered and exited
InstrumentES, NQ, EUR/USD, etc.
DirectionLong or Short
Entry PriceExact entry level
Stop LossWhere your stop was placed
TargetYour planned profit target
Exit PriceWhere you actually exited
P&LProfit or loss in dollars/points
Setup TypeBreakout, pullback, reversal, etc.
NotesWhy you took the trade, what happened
Emotion Score1–10 rating of how disciplined you were

Weekly Review Process

Daily entries are only half the work. The real value comes from your weekly review:

  1. Calculate your win rate, average win, and average loss for the week
  2. Identify which setup types performed best and worst
  3. Review any trades where you deviated from your plan
  4. Note any emotional patterns — did you trade better in the morning or afternoon?
  5. Set one specific goal for the following week based on your findings

Metrics to Track Over Time

  • Win Rate: Percentage of winning trades (aim for 40–60%)
  • Profit Factor: Total wins ÷ Total losses (anything above 1.5 is strong)
  • Average R-Multiple: Average profit in terms of risk units
  • Best Setup: Which trade type generates the most consistent profit
  • Worst Time of Day: When do most of your losses occur?

Journaling for Prop Firm Evaluations

During an evaluation, your journal becomes even more important. It helps you stay accountable to your rules, identify if you're creeping toward the daily loss limit, and review whether your position sizing is consistent.

Pro tip: Screenshot your chart at entry and exit for every trade during your evaluation. Reviewing these screenshots during your weekly review will reveal more than any written notes alone.

Bottom Line

The trading journal is the highest-ROI habit you can build as a prop trader. It costs you 5 minutes per trade and can save you months of repeated mistakes. Start today — your future funded account depends on it.
