Risk Management: The #1 Rule for Prop Traders

Master position sizing, drawdown management, and stop placement to pass evaluations and protect your capital.

Risk management is the foundation of successful prop trading. While many traders focus on finding winning strategies, professional traders know that managing risk is what separates winners from those who blow accounts.

The 2% Rule: Your Most Important Tool

The 2% rule is the cornerstone of professional risk management: never risk more than 2% of your account on a single trade.

Example: If your account is $100,000, risk no more than $2,000 per trade. Even with 10 consecutive losses, you've only lost 20% of your account — survivable with proper management.

Understanding Drawdown

Every prop firm has a drawdown limit — the maximum your account can lose from peak balance. Typical limits are:

  • Daily Drawdown: Loss on any single trading day (usually 5–10%)
  • Maximum Drawdown: Total cumulative loss from peak (usually 10–20%)

Proper position sizing ensures you stay within drawdown limits even during losing streaks.

The Risk-Reward Ratio

Never take a trade unless your potential profit is at least 2x your potential loss.

Setup: Stop loss 50 pips away → Target must be at least 100 pips away
Result: Even a 50% win rate keeps you profitable long-term.

Money Management Rules for Evaluations

  1. Never risk more than 2% per trade — Evaluation accounts have tight drawdown limits
  2. Scale into winners — Once profitable, gradually increase position size
  3. Avoid revenge trading — A single bad trade can end your evaluation
  4. Use daily loss stops — Stop trading after losing 2–3% in a day
  5. Keep a trading journal — Track every trade to identify patterns

Common Risk Management Mistakes

❌ Adding to losing positions — Violates the 2% rule and increases risk

❌ Moving stops arbitrarily — Reduces discipline and inflates losses

❌ Ignoring drawdown limits — The most common way traders blow funded accounts

❌ Over-leveraging — Using too many contracts magnifies losses dangerously

Bottom Line

Risk management isn't flashy, but it's what separates professional traders from gamblers. Master the 2% rule, respect drawdown limits, and use hard stops on every trade — and profitable trading will follow.
